Rental kitchens tend to look the same: dated tiles, one ceiling light and not enough storage. You can't renovate, but you can change a lot without a drill, a contractor or your landlord's permission. Every idea below is removable when you move out.

Before you start, read your lease and test any adhesive product on a hidden spot first. Painted walls and older tiles react differently, and a quick test saves you a surprise at move-out.

## Peel-and-stick backsplash

Nothing changes a rental kitchen faster than the backsplash. Peel-and-stick tiles go right over the existing wall or tile, look surprisingly real, and come off when you leave.

**Pick a style that suits the kitchen.** Classic white subway tile is the safest choice and makes a small kitchen look brighter. Zellige-style tiles add texture. Warm terracotta or soft green hexagons give a plain kitchen real personality.

**Prep matters more than the product.** Clean the wall with a degreaser, let it dry completely and start from a level line, not the counter, which is rarely perfectly straight. Press each tile firmly and smooth from the center outward.

<ProductCard name="Peel-and-stick subway tile backsplash">
  Thicker tiles with a slight 3D surface look more like real ceramic. Order a sample first to check the color in your kitchen light.
</ProductCard>

Avoid placing tiles directly behind a gas stove. Most peel-and-stick products aren't made for direct heat, so check the product's guidance and leave that section bare if you're unsure.

## Stick-on under-cabinet lighting

Most rental kitchens have one overhead light, which casts shadows exactly where you're chopping. Battery-powered or rechargeable LED light bars stick under the upper cabinets with adhesive or magnets and switch on with a touch or motion sensor.

<ProductCard name="Rechargeable under-cabinet LED light bars">
  Motion-sensor models switch on when you walk up and off when you leave, so the battery lasts longer.
</ProductCard>

Choose a warm white light (around 2700K to 3000K) so the kitchen feels cozy rather than clinical.

## Adhesive rails and hooks

A rail across the backsplash with a few S-hooks holds wooden spoons, a whisk, a ladle or even a small hanging herb pot. It clears a drawer and keeps everyday tools within reach of the stove.

Look for rails and hooks with adhesive rated for the weight you plan to hang, and keep heavy pans off them. Light utensils, mugs and towels are the sweet spot.

## Swap the cabinet hardware

Changing knobs and pulls is one of the easiest ways to make cabinets look new. It only needs a screwdriver, and you can put the original hardware back before you move out. Keep the old pieces in a labeled bag.

Measure the distance between the screw holes on your existing pulls before you buy new ones, so they fit the same holes.

## Add storage without installing anything

Rentals rarely have enough storage, but plenty of options need no installation at all:

- **Over-the-sink dish racks** turn the space above the sink into a drying station.
- **Over-the-door organizers** hold spices, wraps and snacks on the inside of a pantry door.
- **Tension rods** inside a cabinet hold cutting boards upright or spray bottles by their triggers.
- **A slim rolling cart** fits the gap between the fridge and the counter.
- **Freestanding shelves** on the counter add a second level for spices or mugs.

## Small styling upgrades

The last layer is the easiest. A washable runner rug on the floor, a few matching canisters on the counter, a plant on the windowsill and new dish towels make a plain rental kitchen feel personal. None of it needs tools, and all of it comes with you when you move.

## Before you move out

Remove adhesive products slowly, pulling parallel to the wall rather than straight out. A hair dryer on a low setting softens most adhesives. Put the original cabinet hardware back, and take photos of the kitchen once everything is removed.
